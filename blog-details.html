How Ghanaian SMEs can cut cyber risk
without a full security team

SHARE:

Facebook
Twitter
Instagram

Start with the risks that actually matter

Most growing businesses in Ghana do not need a large security department to reduce cyber risk. They need a clear baseline: who can access what, which devices are trusted, and how quickly someone notices when something looks wrong. Without that foundation, tools pile up while exposure stays high.

We usually begin with identity and access, endpoint protection, backups you can restore, and a simple incident playbook. Those four moves close the gaps attackers exploit most often — compromised accounts, unpatched machines, ransomware, and slow response when staff report suspicious activity.

Controls that fit a lean IT bench

Multi-factor authentication on email and finance systems, least-privilege admin rights, and regular reviews of shared passwords go a long way. Pair that with patching schedules for servers and endpoints, and you remove many of the easy entry points without hiring a full SOC.

Monitoring does not have to mean a 24/7 operations centre on day one. Start with alerts on failed logins, unusual mailbox rules, and backup failures — then expand as your maturity grows. RDMconsults helps teams prioritise what to watch based on how you actually work.

Make security part of everyday operations

Policy only works when people can follow it. Short training on phishing, clear channels for reporting incidents, and vendor access rules keep security practical. Document the basics so new hires and contractors do not reinvent risky habits.

When you are ready to go further, we layer in assessments, CCTV and physical security where needed, and managed support so improvements stick — instead of becoming a one-off project.

You do not need a full security team to get safer. You need the right baseline, owned by someone, reviewed regularly, and supported when something breaks.

Ama Mensah Security Advisor, RDMconsults

What good looks like in 90 days

In the first quarter we typically aim for MFA coverage on critical apps, cleaned-up admin accounts, verified backups, and a written response checklist. That is enough to change your risk profile materially while you plan deeper architecture work.
